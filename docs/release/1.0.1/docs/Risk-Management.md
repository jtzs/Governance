# Risk Management

The goal of this document is to guide an organisation in conducting general risk management. However, due to the uniqueness of every organisation, its circumstances, environment, and external factors, the way this document is applied will differ. As such, this document serves only as a guide, and proper risk management should be carried out by trained and certified personnel with knowledge of the organisation's internal and external factors.

## Introduction to Risk Management

Risk management is the process of identifying, assessing, mitigating, accepting, and monitoring risks to the organisation. Risk management helps to minimise organisational risks and reduce their impact on the organisation. It also informs organisational leadership of the existence of risks and, most importantly, supports the acceptance of residual risks by organisational leadership.

## Risk Management Definitions

- Risk
  - Definition: An uncertainty or possibility of a negative outcome.
  - E.g. When using a vehicle to travel, there is a risk of vehicle breakdown.
- Vulnerability
  - Definition: A weakness, typically in IT systems, applications, processes, or physical arrangements.
  - E.g. Operating system and application maintainers typically release lists of known vulnerabilities to their customers, together with ways to fix them. Loose screws or improperly maintained machinery could also be vulnerabilities.
- Threat
  - Definition: An internal or external factor that can cause damage or disruption to the organisation.
  - E.g. Bad weather can disrupt an organisation's outdoor activities.
- Impact
  - Definition: The potential negative effect on an organisation, typically resulting from a risk being realised.
  - E.g. Loss of reputation or trust, cancellation of events, harm to people, or damage to equipment.
- Control
  - Definition: Actions or steps taken to reduce the likelihood of a risk occurring or to lessen its impact on the organisation.
  - E.g. Wet weather plans allow activities to continue in the event of bad weather.
- Likelihood
  - Definition: The probability that a risk will occur.
  - E.g. The probability of injury during rough outdoor games is high.
- Criticality
  - Definition: The importance of an asset, process, activity, service, or data item to the organisation.
  - E.g. Data that is critical to the organisation may severely disrupt operations if it is lost.

## Risk Management Process

1. Identification of Risks
2. Assessment of Risks
3. Mitigation of Risks
4. Acceptance of Residual Risks
5. Monitoring and Review

### 1. Identification of Risks

- Identify the organisation's assets, activities, and services.
- Determine the criticality of each asset, activity, or service.
- Identify the risks associated with those assets, activities, and services.
- Consider relevant threats and vulnerabilities.
- Record identified risks in a risk register.
- Prioritise risks based on their likelihood and impact.

### 2. Assessment of Risks

- Assess each identified risk by considering:
  - the likelihood of the risk occurring
  - the potential impact if the risk occurs
- Use a consistent method for evaluating and rating risks.
- Determine which risks require immediate treatment and which can be monitored.

### 3. Mitigation of Risks

- Risks are typically treated through one or more of the following approaches:
  - Avoidance
  - Reduction
  - Transference
  - Acceptance
- Risk treatment activities should be documented as part of the organisation's risk management process.
- Risk treatment plans should be reviewed regularly, typically at least annually.

### 4. Acceptance of Residual Risks

- When risks cannot be further mitigated in a practical or cost-effective manner, the remaining residual risks need to be accepted.
- Residual risks should be escalated to organisational leadership, typically the board or senior management, for awareness and formal acceptance.
- Residual risk acceptance should be reviewed regularly, typically at least annually.

### 5. Monitoring and Review

- Risks should be monitored on an ongoing basis to identify changes in likelihood, impact, or effectiveness of controls.
- The risk register should be kept up to date.
- Reviews should also be performed after significant incidents, major organisational changes, or changes in the external environment.
- Risk management should be treated as a continuous process rather than a one-time exercise.
